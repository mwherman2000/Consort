AIP-Friendly Greek-Style Snack Wrap
(adapted from eggs.ab.ca's Greek-Style Snack Wrap; serves 2)

Ingredients:

- 2 beaten eggs
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1/4 tsp salt and black pepper
- 1/4 tsp dried oregano
- 1 cup baby spinach
- 2 tbsp dairy-free tzatziki (quick version below)
- 2 gluten-free tortillas, 7-inch/18 cm (cassava-flour or coconut-flour)
- 1/4 cup diced cucumber (replacing the cherry tomatoes)
- 2 tbsp dairy-free feta-style crumble (coconut- or almond-based), or 1 tbsp chopped olives

Quick dairy-free tzatziki (makes about 1/4 cup): 1/4 cup coconut- or cashew-based plain yogurt, 2 tbsp grated cucumber (squeezed dry), 1 small garlic clove (minced), 1 tsp lemon juice, a pinch of fresh or dried dill. Stir together and set aside.

Instructions:

1. Heat the olive oil in a large nonstick skillet over medium heat. Add the beaten eggs with the salt, pepper, and oregano. Reduce to medium-low and cook gently, moving the spatula across the bottom and sides of the skillet as the mixture forms large, soft curds.
2. Mix in the spinach and continue cooking until the eggs are thickened as you like and the spinach has wilted slightly.
3. Warm the tortillas briefly — a few seconds per side in a dry skillet, or about 10-15 seconds in the microwave — so they're pliable and won't crack when rolled.
4. Spread the tzatziki down the center of each tortilla, leaving about a 1-inch border at top and bottom.
5. Layer on the egg-and-spinach mixture, then top with the diced cucumber and dairy-free feta (or olives).
6. Fold the bottom edge up over the filling, fold in the sides, and roll tightly upward.

Cook time: about 5 minutes.

_A note from the home cook:_ the original's cherry tomatoes and feta gave it that classic Greek tang — this version leans on the cucumber's freshness and a good dairy-free tzatziki to carry that same brightness, so nothing should feel like it's missing. If you can't find a dairy-free feta you like, chopped kalamata olives do a lovely job standing in for that salty, briny note.

_(Black pepper and olive oil needed no substitution — pepper isn't a nightshade despite the name, and olive oil isn't a seed oil.)_
